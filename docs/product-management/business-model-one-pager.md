# One page. What the system does, what it costs, what it is worth

A credential is an institution's signature in a form a machine can check. This page sets out what
that gives each party, and what each party pays for it.

## Institution — the organisation that issues the credentials

**What it can do**
- Issue a student's qualification and transcript as a digital credential the student holds on their phone, instead of printing a document or emailing a PDF.
- Let employers, universities and other organisations confirm that credential in seconds, without anyone contacting the institution.
- Cancel a credential when it has to. A cancelled credential stops verifying everywhere, immediately.
- See everything it has issued in the management portal, and let its own student systems issue directly with an API key.

**What it costs**
- A subscription to the platform, and a fee for each credential issued.
- A one-off piece of work to connect its student record system and agree what a credential should say.
- Either keeping its signing keys safe itself, or paying the platform to manage them.
- Training for registrar staff, and helping students who lose their phone.

**What it is worth**
- Registrar time: a manual transcript request takes 10–30 minutes of staff time. A check by an employer or another university takes none of it.
- Postage and courier: often £5–£45 internationally, gone for anything confirmed digitally.
- No printing, scanning, filing, storing or redacting documents.
- Storage for credentials: nothing. They are never held on a server, so the cost does not grow with the number issued.
- Less fraud: a forged or edited document fails verification, instead of arriving in the post looking convincing.
- A new revenue line: transcript and certification fees delivered digitally at a fraction of the old handling cost, plus alumni self-service.
- Students receive their qualification when it is awarded, not weeks later — something the institution can advertise.

## Quals — the platform

**What it does**
- Runs the whole service: issuing credentials on the institution's behalf, the wallet app students hold, the verification service, the public revocation list, the management portal and the share page.
- Onboards institutions, manages their signing keys, gives them portal access and API keys.
- Serves the organisations that check credentials — My Jobs, Trust University, and any employer or admissions office.
- Publishes the signed revocation list, so anyone can check whether a credential has been cancelled without asking the institution.

**What it earns**
- From institutions: a subscription, plus a fee per credential issued.
- From checking organisations: a fee per verification, or an annual bundle.
- Accreditation and listing fees, once enough institutions are on board for the registry to be worth subscribing to.
- Managed signing and hosting, sold as an add-on.
- Premium holder transactions: expedited delivery, legalisation, certified translation, a lifelong vault.

**What it costs**
- Hosting the services, storage and email. Credentials and verifications cost almost nothing in infrastructure — the money goes on people, not servers.
- Engineering: integrations, standards, security.
- Onboarding and supporting each institution and its students, which is the real cost.
- Security operations: key custody, key rotation, monitoring, incident response.
- Keeping the revocation list available, because a check cannot complete without it.
- Sales and partnerships.

## The holder — the student or graduate

**What they get**
- They own the record, instead of asking the institution for access to it.
- They show only what is asked. A landlord checking that they are a student does not receive their grades.
- No fees, no couriers, no waiting.
- It travels with them: between jobs, courses and countries, for life.
- Faster outcomes — a job offer, an admission, a visa, a loan, resolved in minutes.

**What it costs them**
- Nothing to hold or share. They pay only for extras, such as expedited official delivery, translation or legalisation.
- They need a phone and an app.
- If they lose the phone, the credential needs re-issuing.
